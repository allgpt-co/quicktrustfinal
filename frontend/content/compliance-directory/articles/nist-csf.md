## Use outcomes to organize cybersecurity work

The [NIST Cybersecurity Framework](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) helps organizations describe and manage cybersecurity risk. Version 2.0 is intended for organizations across sizes and sectors. Its six functions are Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover. These organize outcomes rather than prescribe one identical technical implementation for every business.

[NIST's FAQs](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework/faqs) explain that most organizations use CSF voluntarily. NIST does not operate a CSF certification program. A supplier's statement that it uses CSF should therefore lead to questions about scope, outcomes and evidence, rather than an assumption that NIST has issued a certificate. Separate contracts and sector rules may reference CSF and need their own applicability review.

## Turn a profile into an improvement plan

NIST's [quick-start guides](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework/quick-start-guides) explain organizational profiles and other implementation resources. A Current Profile describes present outcomes; a Target Profile helps establish desired outcomes. Comparing them supports prioritization.

For an internal planning session, agree which services and business units are included. Invite the people who understand operational dependencies as well as the security team. Record the evidence behind each assessment and identify decisions that need an accountable owner. Then attach a practical next step to each prioritized gap.

Revisit the profile when the organization changes its systems, customers or risk assumptions. A completed spreadsheet can be a useful record, but it should not be treated as proof that every relevant outcome remains achieved indefinitely. The checklist supplied here supports preparation for this process and links back to NIST resources. It does not replace those resources or determine your legal obligations.
