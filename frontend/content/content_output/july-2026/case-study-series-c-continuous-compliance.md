# Illustrative Scenario: Multi-Team Continuous Compliance

> **Illustrative scenario — not a verified customer case study.** This hypothetical example describes proposed work. It reports no actual customer, testimonial, certification, revenue, savings, or delivery timeline.

## The planning situation

A hypothetical SaaS company has several product teams and a shared identity service. Reviews are completed inconsistently because central policies do not clearly assign local implementation responsibilities.

## Proposed implementation approach

### Map ownership

Separate centrally operated controls from product-specific work. Assign one accountable owner for each handoff and document the contributors.

### Rebuild the review cadence

Identify recurring access, supplier, policy and recovery reviews. Define the required records and escalation for missed or incomplete work.

### Track exceptions

Record risk acceptance with an approver, rationale and expiry. Review differences between products instead of hiding them inside a company-wide score.

### Report operational trends

Discuss repeated failures, stale evidence and overdue corrective actions. Use the review to resolve resourcing and ownership decisions.

## Acceptance criteria to verify

- Every recurring control has an operational owner.
- Exceptions have an expiry and a review path.
- Program reporting distinguishes reviewed evidence from unresolved work.

## Apply the example to your organization

Replace the assumptions with your own systems, requirements and owners. Confirm the relevant assessment or legal questions with the responsible specialist. An implementation task is complete only when the agreed behavior and evidence have been reviewed; this scenario does not imply an assessment result.
