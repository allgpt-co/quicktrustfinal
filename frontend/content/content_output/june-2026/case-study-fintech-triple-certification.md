# Illustrative Scenario: Mapping Fintech Framework Controls

> **Illustrative scenario — not a verified customer case study.** This hypothetical example describes proposed work. It reports no actual customer, testimonial, certification, revenue, savings, or delivery timeline.

## The planning situation

A hypothetical fintech organization receives SOC 2, payment-card and ISO 27001 requests from different counterparties. It wants to identify shared controls without assuming that one assessment can satisfy all requests.

## Proposed implementation approach

### Confirm each scope

Record the entity, service, systems and evidence period required for each request. Involve the relevant assessors and payment partners.

### Map shared controls

Review access, change management, supplier oversight and recovery controls for potential reuse. Document why a record supports each mapped requirement.

### Keep distinct obligations visible

Retain payment-specific boundaries and management-system decisions as separate work. Do not assign equivalence based only on similar control names.

### Sequence dependencies

Coordinate implementation, evidence collection and independent review schedules. Escalate conflicting scope assumptions before changing production.

## Acceptance criteria to verify

- Each framework has a confirmed boundary and decision owner.
- Shared evidence has an explicit mapping rationale.
- Framework-specific work remains visible in the backlog.

## Apply the example to your organization

Replace the assumptions with your own systems, requirements and owners. Confirm the relevant assessment or legal questions with the responsible specialist. An implementation task is complete only when the agreed behavior and evidence have been reviewed; this scenario does not imply an assessment result.
