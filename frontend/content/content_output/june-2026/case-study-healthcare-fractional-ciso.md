# Illustrative Scenario: Healthcare Security Leadership Scope

> **Illustrative scenario — not a verified customer case study.** This hypothetical example describes proposed work. It reports no actual customer, testimonial, certification, revenue, savings, or delivery timeline.

## The planning situation

A hypothetical healthcare technology company needs clearer security decision-making but has not established a dedicated leadership role. It is evaluating the responsibilities an external adviser could support.

## Proposed implementation approach

### Identify decision gaps

List unresolved risk, supplier, incident and policy decisions. Separate executive accountability from tasks an adviser or implementation specialist can perform.

### Define the engagement

Specify the advisory scope, meeting cadence, escalation responsibilities and deliverables. Confirm relevant experience without assuming credentials or availability.

### Connect advice to execution

Assign system owners to approved actions and define the evidence required for completion. Keep operational changes within the organization’s approval process.

### Review the operating model

Assess whether decisions are made on time and whether owners maintain the controls. Revisit the engagement as systems and responsibilities change.

## Acceptance criteria to verify

- Decision rights and retained executive responsibilities are clear.
- Advice results in owned, testable actions.
- The staffing choice is evaluated without invented savings or contract outcomes.

## Apply the example to your organization

Replace the assumptions with your own systems, requirements and owners. Confirm the relevant assessment or legal questions with the responsible specialist. An implementation task is complete only when the agreed behavior and evidence have been reviewed; this scenario does not imply an assessment result.
