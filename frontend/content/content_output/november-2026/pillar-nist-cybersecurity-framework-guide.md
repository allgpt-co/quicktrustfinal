# NIST Cybersecurity Framework: Functions and Profiles

## Use outcomes to organize cybersecurity work

The [NIST Cybersecurity Framework](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) helps organizations describe and manage cybersecurity risk. Version 2.0 is intended for organizations across sizes and sectors. Its six functions are Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover. These organize outcomes rather than prescribe one identical technical implementation for every business.

[NIST's FAQs](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework/faqs) explain that most organizations use CSF voluntarily. NIST does not operate a CSF certification program. A supplier's statement that it uses CSF should therefore lead to questions about scope, outcomes and evidence, rather than an assumption that NIST has issued a certificate. Separate contracts and sector rules may reference CSF and need their own applicability review.

## Turn a profile into an improvement plan

NIST's [quick-start guides](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework/quick-start-guides) explain organizational profiles and other implementation resources. A Current Profile describes present outcomes; a Target Profile helps establish desired outcomes. Comparing them supports prioritization.

For an internal planning session, agree which services and business units are included. Invite the people who understand operational dependencies as well as the security team. Record the evidence behind each assessment and identify decisions that need an accountable owner. Then attach a practical next step to each prioritized gap.

Revisit the profile when the organization changes its systems, customers or risk assumptions. A completed spreadsheet can be a useful record, but it should not be treated as proof that every relevant outcome remains achieved indefinitely. The checklist supplied here supports preparation for this process and links back to NIST resources. It does not replace those resources or determine your legal obligations.

## The six functions and the questions they help organize

The functions can be discussed together; they are not a sequence that ends after recovery. NIST's [CSF 2.0 publication](https://www.nist.gov/publications/nist-cybersecurity-framework-csf-20) is the authoritative source for categories and subcategories.

| Function | A useful planning question |
|---|---|
| Govern | Who decides how cybersecurity risk is managed and how those decisions are reviewed? |
| Identify | Which assets, dependencies and risks matter to the organization? |
| Protect | Which safeguards support the services and assets in scope? |
| Detect | How will potentially adverse events be noticed and analyzed? |
| Respond | Who acts when an incident occurs, and how are actions coordinated? |
| Recover | How will affected services be restored and recovery communicated? |

These questions are editorial prompts, not a substitute for the framework's outcomes. Their answers should be specific enough that another person can understand the scope and supporting evidence.

## Profiles and Tiers serve different purposes

A Profile connects outcomes to organizational circumstances and priorities. A Current Profile can distinguish outcomes achieved from work still in progress; a Target Profile expresses the intended destination. NIST's [quick-start guides](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework/quick-start-guides) provide practical resources for developing and using them.

Tiers describe the rigor of cybersecurity risk governance and management practices. Their names are Partial, Risk Informed, Repeatable and Adaptive. They should support a conversation about risk management rather than become a universal target score. A Tier label does not establish SOC 2 readiness, ISO certification or legal compliance.

## Compare frameworks without claiming equivalence

CSF can help organize a broader assurance program, but each regulation, certification or customer requirement retains its own scope and criteria. A mapping is a relationship to investigate, not proof that one implemented safeguard satisfies every mapped requirement.

When comparing CSF with [SOC 2](/soc-2-compliance) or [ISO 27001](/iso-27001-certification), record the version, scope and evidence required for each. Do not assume a fixed percentage of work is already complete. Assessment conclusions depend on the actual controls and the relevant assessor's criteria.
