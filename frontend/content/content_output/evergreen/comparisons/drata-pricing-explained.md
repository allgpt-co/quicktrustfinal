# Drata Pricing: What Is Public, What Is Not, and How to Budget

If you are trying to budget for Drata, the first thing to know is that the vendor does not publish a price. The second thing to know is that this is normal for the category, and there is a reliable way to get a comparable number anyway. This article covers what Drata's site actually says, what usually goes into a compliance platform quote, how to run the sales call so the quote is useful, and how to build a budget that includes the costs the quote leaves out.

Everything stated about Drata below reflects its public site as reviewed on 2 October 2026. Vendors change their pages, so verify directly before relying on this.

## What Drata publishes about pricing

Drata's [pricing page](https://drata.com/pricing) publishes no prices, no tiers and no amounts. The page links to "Contact Sales" and to a demo request. There is no plan comparison table, no named plan levels and no add-on list on the page as reviewed.

| Item | What Drata's pricing page shows |
| --- | --- |
| Dollar amounts | None published |
| Named plan tiers | None published |
| Add-on list | None published |
| Path to a price | Contact Sales link and demo request |

That is the full extent of what can be stated from the vendor's own page. This article does not estimate Drata's prices, and any dollar figure attributed to Drata on a third-party site is that site's claim, not Drata's. Such figures may be outdated, specific to one customer's negotiation, or simply invented.

The absence of published tiers has a practical implication. With a vendor that names plans, you can at least ask "what is in Professional versus Plus". With Drata, the packaging itself has to be discovered on the call, so the question list later in this article matters more, not less.

## Why the number is not public

Quote-based pricing is the norm across compliance automation platforms, and the reasons are structural rather than specific to any one vendor.

**The deal is a combination of variables.** Frameworks, headcount, integrations, modules and services each move the price. Publishing every combination would require a matrix that goes out of date quickly.

**Negotiation is expected.** Enterprise software sales commonly involve discounting against a list price. Not publishing the list keeps that flexibility.

**Customer segments differ widely.** A seed-stage startup and a public company are buying very different scopes, and vendors generally prefer not to show either one the other's number.

**Packaging evolves.** Modules get added, bundled and unbundled over time. A quote reflects current packaging without a public changelog.

The result is that a buyer cannot compare vendors from their websites. Comparison has to happen through structured questions, asked the same way of each vendor, with answers recorded in writing.

## What typically drives a compliance platform quote

The following inputs are typical of how compliance automation platforms price. Drata's page does not state which of these it uses, so treat this as a list of what to ask about rather than a description of Drata's model.

| Driver | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Frameworks in scope | Each framework brings its own control content and audit workflow. A single SOC 2 scope is usually quoted differently from SOC 2 plus ISO 27001 plus HIPAA plus PCI DSS. |
| Employee count | Platforms that track personnel controls, such as training, device compliance and access, often price on headcount. |
| Integrations | Integrations may be unlimited, capped or tiered. Ask which applies and what counts as one integration. |
| Modules and add-ons | Capabilities such as vendor risk management, questionnaire handling, trust pages or risk management may be bundled or sold separately. |
| Services | Onboarding, implementation help and premium support may be included, time-limited or separately priced. |
| Contract term | Term length and payment timing frequently affect the rate. |

**Audit fees are not in the platform quote.** The platform prepares you for an audit; the audit is conducted by an independent licensed CPA firm with its own fee. The [SOC 2 audit cost](/blog/soc2-audit-cost-2026) article covers what shapes that fee. Even where a platform offers an auditor network, you contract with the firm.

## Questions to ask Drata, or any compliance platform vendor

Record answers in a shared document so they can be laid next to other vendors' answers.

### Packaging

- What plans or tiers exist, and what is the difference between them?
- Which capabilities are included in the base price and which are modules or add-ons? Please provide the list in writing.
- Which frameworks are included in this quote, and what does each additional framework cost?

### Variables

- Is the price tied to employee count? How are contractors, part-time staff and service accounts counted?
- If headcount changes during the term, does the price change?
- Are integrations unlimited, and if not, what is the limit?
- Is this a first-year price? What is the renewal policy?

### Implementation

- What onboarding is included, for how long, and who delivers it?
- Who implements missing controls, configures integrations and adapts policies: our team, yours or a partner?
- If a partner is required, is that cost in this quote?

### Audit and assurance

- Is the audit fee included? If there is an auditor network, do those firms set their own fees?
- Does choosing an auditor inside or outside the network affect the platform price?

### Exit

- If we do not renew, how do we export our evidence, control history and policies?
- What is the data retention policy after termination?

## How to budget for the full cost

A compliance budget that contains only the platform subscription is incomplete. Build it with the following lines, and fill in every one even if the answer is an estimate you flag as such.

| Budget line | What to include |
| --- | --- |
| Platform subscription | Quoted price for the full term, with the renewal policy applied |
| Modules and add-ons | Everything you will actually use, priced for the full term |
| Internal implementation labor | Engineering, security and operations hours to implement controls, connect systems, write policies and gather evidence, priced at a loaded internal rate |
| External implementation services | Consultant or partner fees where the platform does not include hands-on implementation |
| Audit fees | Independent auditor fees per framework per year |
| Ongoing operation | Hours to maintain integrations, run recurring controls such as access reviews, and keep evidence current |
| Switching cost | The effort to migrate if you change platforms later |

The internal labor line deserves the most attention. A platform's main output in the early weeks is a list of controls that are not yet passing. Each one is a task for someone on your team. If the team does not have the capacity, the task either waits, delaying the audit, or gets handed to an outside party at a cost. Either way it belongs in the budget. The audit is performed by an independent licensed CPA firm, which charges separately. Confirm the arrangement on the sales call, but budget the audit fee as its own line for any compliance platform.
