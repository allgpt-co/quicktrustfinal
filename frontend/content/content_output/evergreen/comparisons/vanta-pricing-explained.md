# Vanta Pricing Explained: Plans, Add-ons and What to Ask Before You Buy

Searching for Vanta pricing usually produces two kinds of results: pages that repeat guesses from other pages, and the vendor's own pricing page, which does not list a price. This article sticks to what Vanta publishes, explains why compliance platforms price the way they do, and gives you a structured way to get a real number and compare it against alternatives.

Everything stated about Vanta below reflects its public pricing page as reviewed on 2 October 2026. Vanta can change its page at any time, so check it directly before relying on this summary.

This article does not estimate what Vanta charges. Any figure you find elsewhere that is not on Vanta's own page is a third-party claim, and it may be out of date or wrong.

## Why compliance platforms quote rather than publish

Quote-based pricing is common across this category, not unique to Vanta. There are a few consistent reasons.

**The product is sold as a bundle of variables.** Frameworks, headcount, integrations, add-ons and services can each change the price, and the combinations multiply quickly. Vendors prefer to price the combination rather than publish a matrix.

**Discounting is part of the sales motion.** A published list price constrains negotiation. A quote does not.

**Buyers are segmented.** A startup pursuing a first SOC 2 and an enterprise managing several frameworks represent very different revenue, and vendors would rather not show one segment the other's price.

**Packaging changes frequently.** Add-on lists and plan boundaries shift as products evolve. A quote reflects today's packaging without the vendor having to maintain a public price history.

None of this is improper. It does mean that the burden of comparison falls on the buyer, and the only way to carry that burden is to ask the same structured questions of every vendor.

## What typically drives a compliance platform quote

Across the compliance automation category, quotes are generally shaped by a recognizable set of inputs. Vanta's page confirms some of these in the form of plan tiers and add-on labels; the rest are typical of the category and should be confirmed on your call.

| Driver | Why it affects price |
| --- | --- |
| Frameworks in scope | Each framework adds content, mappings and audit workflows. SOC 2 alone is usually priced differently from SOC 2 plus ISO 27001 plus HIPAA. |
| Employee count | Many platforms monitor people as well as systems, tracking training, device compliance and access, so headcount becomes a pricing variable. |
| Integrations | Some vendors include all integrations; others tier them or limit the number connected. |
| Add-ons | Capabilities labelled as add-ons are priced separately from the plan. On Vanta's page, these include questionnaire automation, security reviews, continuous monitoring, risk customization and third-party risk management. |
| Services | Onboarding, implementation assistance and dedicated support may be included, tiered or sold separately. |
| Contract term | Multi-year commitments and payment timing often affect the quoted rate. |

One more item does not appear in platform quotes at all, and buyers routinely forget it: **audit fees are separate.** A compliance platform prepares you for an audit. The audit itself is performed by an independent licensed CPA firm, which charges its own fee. The [SOC 2 audit cost](/blog/soc2-audit-cost-2026) article explains what shapes that fee. Some platforms maintain auditor networks, but the engagement and the invoice are with the firm.

## Questions to ask on the sales call

Take these into the call and record the answers. The point is not to catch anyone out; it is to produce a quote you can compare line by line against another vendor.

### Plans and add-ons

- Which plan are you quoting, and what specifically is included in it?
- Which of the capabilities we discussed are add-ons? Please list every add-on with its price.
- Are questionnaire automation, security reviews, continuous monitoring, risk customization and third-party risk management included in this quote or priced separately?
- If we start on one plan and add a framework later, what changes?

### Pricing variables

- Is the price based on employee count? What counts as an employee: contractors, part-time staff, service accounts?
- What happens to the price if headcount grows mid-contract?
- Are all integrations included? Is there a cap?
- Is the quoted price for the first year only, and what is the renewal uplift policy?

### Services and implementation

- What onboarding is included, for how long, and delivered by whom?
- Who configures integrations, writes policies and implements missing controls: your team, ours or a partner?
- If a partner is involved, is that cost in this quote?
- What ongoing support is included after onboarding?

### Audit

- Is the audit fee included? If not, do you have an auditor network, and do those auditors set their own fees?
- Does using an auditor from your network change the platform price?

### Exit

- What happens to our evidence archive and control history if we do not renew?
- What are the export formats?

## How to compare total cost of ownership

The subscription is one line in a larger budget. A fair comparison between vendors, or between a vendor and doing it another way, should count every line.

| Cost line | What to include |
| --- | --- |
| Platform subscription | The quoted plan price for the full contract term, including any renewal uplift |
| Add-ons | Every add-on you will actually need, priced for the full term |
| Internal implementation labor | Hours from engineering, security and operations to configure integrations, implement controls, write and adapt policies and collect evidence. Price these hours at a loaded internal rate. |
| External implementation services | Consultants or partner fees if the platform does not include implementation |
| Audit fees | The independent auditor's fee, per framework, per year |
| Ongoing operation | Hours to maintain integrations, run access reviews, keep evidence current and manage renewals |
| Switching cost | Migration effort if you later change platforms, including evidence export |

Internal labor is the line most often left blank, and it is often the largest. A platform that identifies a hundred failing controls has created a hundred engineering tasks. If your team cannot absorb them, the real cost is either delayed audit or paid implementation help, and either should appear in the comparison.
