# QuickTrust vs Vanta: Which Compliance Platform Is Right for Your Company?

Both QuickTrust and Vanta help companies get compliant — but they take fundamentally different approaches to the problem. Vanta automates evidence collection and surfaces compliance gaps. QuickTrust does all of that, and then its engineers actually fix the gaps for you.

If you're evaluating compliance automation tools and wondering whether Vanta or QuickTrust is the right fit, this guide gives you a fair, detailed comparison across pricing, features, engineer support, framework coverage, and deployment model. Neither tool is universally better — the right choice depends on what stage your company is at and how much internal security engineering capacity you have.

## Why This Comparison Matters

Vanta is one of the most recognized names in compliance automation, and for good reason — it has a well-designed interface, a large library of integrations, and a strong brand reputation. But compliance automation tools like Vanta operate on an important assumption: that your engineering team will do the actual implementation work once the gaps are identified.

For many startups and fast-growing SaaS companies, that assumption breaks down quickly. Your engineering team is building the product. They don't have two to three months to rearchitect IAM policies, set up a SIEM, implement SAST/DAST pipelines, write 40 information security policies from scratch, and prepare evidence packs — all while shipping new features.

QuickTrust was built to close this loop. The platform maps your gaps and then QuickTrust's in-house Security and DevOps engineers implement the controls in your infrastructure.

## Where Vanta Wins

To give you an honest picture, here are areas where Vanta genuinely excels:

**Integration breadth.** Vanta boasts 750+ native integrations with cloud providers, SaaS tools, and infrastructure services. If you're already using a wide stack of third-party tools, Vanta's automated data pulls from those services are a significant time-saver.

**Brand recognition and auditor trust.** Vanta is one of the most established names in the space. Many auditors are familiar with Vanta's evidence portal format, which can slightly reduce friction during the audit process.

**UI polish and onboarding.** Vanta has invested heavily in its user interface. The dashboard is clean, well-organized, and designed for non-security professionals to navigate without heavy training.

**Established customer base and case studies.** Vanta has a large customer base across hundreds of verticals, providing a wealth of public case studies and peer references.

**Continuous monitoring out of the box.** Vanta's monitoring engine runs continuously and flags regressions in real time, which is valuable for maintaining SOC 2 Type II compliance over time.

## Where QuickTrust Wins

**Engineers are included — not optional.** This is the fundamental difference. When QuickTrust identifies a gap, its in-house Security and DevOps engineers implement the fix in your cloud environment (AWS, GCP, Azure). You are not left with a gap report and a wish.

**Open-source with self-hosted option.** QuickTrust's platform is licensed under AGPL v3 and can be self-hosted. For companies in highly regulated industries — healthcare, finance, defense — or those with strict data sovereignty requirements, the ability to run the platform entirely within their own infrastructure is not a nice-to-have. It is a requirement. Vanta has no self-hosted option.

**ISO 42001 (AI governance) support.** QuickTrust supports ISO 42001, the international standard for AI management systems. As regulators increasingly scrutinize AI systems, this is a differentiator Vanta does not yet match.

**No per-seat pricing.** Vanta's pricing scales with the number of employees and integrations, which means compliance costs keep rising as your company grows. QuickTrust's pricing is package-based and does not penalize growth.

**AI-native architecture.** QuickTrust is built on LangGraph AI agents and LiteLLM from the ground up — not a traditional SaaS tool with AI features bolted on. The AI engine generates controls, maps questionnaire responses to policy sections, and identifies gaps across your entire framework simultaneously.

**Questionnaire-to-policy mapping.** When a prospect sends you a security questionnaire, QuickTrust maps each question to your existing policies and controls, flags gaps, and creates a reusable response library. This feature directly addresses a revenue-blocking problem. Vanta does not offer this natively.

**Full remediation loop.** Vanta identifies what is broken. QuickTrust identifies what is broken and fixes it. For a startup where engineers are resource-constrained, this difference determines whether compliance actually gets completed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**1. Can QuickTrust replace Vanta completely, or do both tools serve different purposes?**

QuickTrust covers the full compliance lifecycle — gap assessment, control implementation, evidence collection, audit coordination, and continuous maintenance — that Vanta addresses only partially (evidence collection and gap surfacing). For most companies, QuickTrust is a complete replacement, not a complementary tool.

**2. How does QuickTrust achieve a 100% audit pass rate?**

QuickTrust's engineers do not just advise — they implement. The platform's AI agents map every requirement and then in-house Security and DevOps engineers execute the controls in your infrastructure and validate that evidence is captured correctly. Before the audit begins, every control is verified. This end-to-end ownership of implementation is why QuickTrust has maintained a 100% audit pass rate across 100+ engagements.

**3. Vanta has 750+ integrations. Does QuickTrust support the same tools?**

QuickTrust's integration library is actively growing. For most cloud-native startups (AWS, GCP, Azure, GitHub, Okta, Slack, Jira), QuickTrust connects to the core tools where evidence lives. The key distinction is that QuickTrust's engineers also set up and configure these tools properly — so you are not just monitoring a misconfigured environment, you are operating a correctly configured one.

**4. Is QuickTrust's open-source version fully functional, or is it a limited "community edition"?**

QuickTrust is fully open-source under AGPL v3. The open-source repository includes SOC 2 Type II seeded data (9 domains, 33 requirements), 25 control templates across 8 security domains, and 20 evidence templates. Companies can self-host the complete platform. The implementation engineering services are a commercial offering separate from the open-source platform.

**5. How long does it take to get audit-ready with QuickTrust compared to Vanta?**

QuickTrust customers are typically audit-ready in 6–10 weeks. With Vanta, the platform itself may be set up quickly, but the engineering implementation work that follows typically adds three to six months depending on your team's capacity. QuickTrust compresses the timeline because the engineers are already part of the engagement.
